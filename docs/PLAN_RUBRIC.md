## Goal weighting → intensity and rep ranges

- If the top-ranked goal is `strength`, then prescribe `primary` at 3–6 sets of 1–5 reps at 80–92% 1RM or RPE 7–9, `secondary` at 3–4x4–6 at 72–80% or RPE 7–8, and `accessory` at 2–3x8–12 at RPE 6–8.
- If the top-ranked goal is `hypertrophy`, then prescribe `primary` at 3–5x5–8 at 70–80% or RPE 7–9, `secondary` at 3–4x8–12 at RPE 7–9, and `accessory` at 2–4x12–20 at RPE 7–10, keeping 0–3 reps in reserve on every working set.
- If the top-ranked goal is `endurance`, then prescribe `primary` at 3–4x6–10 at 60–70% or RPE 6–7 for strength retention only, and put the training stress in `conditioning` at 20–50 minutes per session tracked by `time`, `distance`, or `cal`.
- If the top-ranked goal is `mobility`, then cap all loaded work at RPE 7, prescribe `primary` and `secondary` at 3x8–12 through the fullest pain-free range available, and place 2–4 subroutines per session in `warmup` and `cooldown`.
- If the top-ranked goal is `skill work`, then prescribe `primary` as 5–8 sets of 1–3 reps at 60–80% or RPE 5–7 with the note "stop the set when speed or position degrades", and never take a skill item past RPE 8.
- If the top-ranked goal is unrecognised free text, then default to the `hypertrophy` bands above (`primary` 3–5x5–8 at RPE 7–9, `accessory` 2–4x12–20) and add one `accessory` item per week whose name contains the athlete's own goal wording.
- If the top goal's weight is 70 or higher, then give it 60–70% of total weekly hard sets, allow only one other goal to appear in `primary`, and hold rep ranges inside that goal's band for all 6–12 weeks.
- If the top goal's weight is 30–50, then give it 35–45% of weekly hard sets and let the second and third goals set the `secondary` and `accessory` rep ranges respectively, so the plan reads as a blend rather than a specialisation.
- If the top two goals are within 10 weight points of each other, then alternate their bands by session — goal A drives `primary` on days 1 and 3, goal B on days 2 and 4 — rather than averaging the rep ranges into a single 6–10 mush.
- If three or more goals sit within 15 points, then run every `primary` at 4x5 at 75–80%, every `secondary` at 3x8–10 at RPE 7–8, every `accessory` at 3x12–15, and add 2 `conditioning` items per week; do not attempt to specialise.

## Goal mix → section emphasis

- If any plan is written, then always include at least 1 `warmup` item and 1 `cooldown` item per session, 5–10 minutes each, regardless of goal mix.
- If `strength` or `skill work` weighs 50 or more, then prescribe 1–2 `primary` items, 1–2 `secondary`, 2–3 `accessory`, and 0–1 `conditioning` per session.
- If `hypertrophy` weighs 50 or more, then prescribe 1 `primary`, 2 `secondary`, 4–6 `accessory`, and 0–1 `conditioning` per session, with `accessory` carrying the majority of weekly sets.
- If `endurance` weighs 40 or more, then give `conditioning` 3–5 items per week totalling 90–180 minutes and cut `accessory` to 1–2 items per session to protect recovery.
- If `endurance` weighs 15–39, then prescribe 2 `conditioning` items per week of 15–25 minutes each — real, progressed volume, not a finisher.
- If `endurance` weighs under 15 or is absent, then prescribe at most 1 `conditioning` item per session at 6–10 minutes as a token finisher, tracked by `time` or `cal`.
- If `mobility` weighs 25 or more, then prescribe 3–5 subroutines per week in `warmup` or `cooldown`, each with a description under 300 characters naming positions and hold times (e.g. 3x60s per side).
- If `mobility` weighs under 25, then express it only as 1–2 standing subroutines repeated every session, and do not spend `primary` or `secondary` slots on it.
- If two goals both need `primary` slots in one session, then place the higher-weighted goal's item first in `primary` and add a note on the second item reading "perform after full recovery, 3+ min".

## Experience level → volume, complexity, progression

- If experience is `beginner`, then prescribe 8–12 hard sets per muscle region per week, 3–5 distinct exercises per session, and no more than 6 distinct exercises total in the plan's `primary` and `secondary` sections combined.
- If experience is `intermediate`, then prescribe 12–18 hard sets per muscle region per week and 5–8 distinct exercises per session.
- If experience is `advanced`, then prescribe 16–22 hard sets per muscle region per week for prioritised regions and 10–12 for the rest, with 6–10 distinct exercises per session.
- If experience is `beginner`, then restrict `primary` to bilateral, low-skill patterns (goblet squat, trap-bar or Romanian deadlift, dumbbell or machine press, machine row) and progress by adding load each week at fixed sets and reps: `3x5` across all weeks with a note "+2.5 kg upper / +5 kg lower per week while all reps are clean".
- If experience is `intermediate`, then progress by reps within a block then load between blocks: weeks 1–4 as `4x6`, `4x7`, `4x8`, `4x6 @+5%`, expressed as a per-week varying prescription.
- If experience is `advanced`, then progress by intensity with falling volume inside a block: weeks 1–4 as `5x5 @72%`, `5x5 @75%`, `4x4 @80%`, `3x3 @85%`, and vary set counts as well as load.
- If experience is `beginner`, then never prescribe percentage-based loads for the first 3 weeks — use RPE 6–7 and rep targets only, since no reliable 1RM exists.
- If experience is `advanced` and `skill work` or `strength` is top-ranked, then permit 1 `realization` week containing a single-rep test at 92–100% 1RM; otherwise omit maximal testing entirely.
- If experience is `beginner` and the athlete has more than 4 training days available, then still write only 3–4 training days and leave the remainder unprogrammed rather than inflating volume.

## Age → selection, volume, recovery

- If age is under 18, then prescribe no maximal (1RM) testing, express all load as RPE 5–8 or rep targets, cap `primary` at 3–4x5–8, and keep weekly hard sets per region at 8–14.
- If age is 18–39, then apply the experience-level defaults unchanged and make no age-based reduction of any kind.
- If age is 40–54, then keep intensity bands and weekly set volume identical to the 18–39 defaults, but cap sessions at 60–75 minutes and hold hard `primary` days to 2 per week with 48 hours between them.
- If age is 55–69, then keep the same rep ranges and %1RM bands, reduce weekly hard sets per region by roughly 15% (e.g. 16 → 13), extend `warmup` to 10–12 minutes including 2–3 ramping sets on the `primary` lift, and add 1 extra rest day versus the requested split if 5 or more days were requested.
- If age is 70 or above, then still prescribe loaded resistance work at 70–85% or RPE 7–8 for 2–3x5–8 on `primary`, add 2 sessions per week of `conditioning` at 10–20 minutes, and add 2 balance or single-leg `accessory` items per week at 2–3x8–12 per side.
- If age is 55 or above, then add one dedicated power item per session at 3x3–5 at 30–50% 1RM moved with maximal intent (jump, throw, sled push), because rate of force development declines fastest and must be trained, not removed.
- If age is 40 or above, then extend any new movement's introduction over 2–3 weeks at RPE 6–7 before loading it hard, since connective tissue adapts more slowly than muscle.
- If age is 40 or above, then do NOT remove barbell lifts, do NOT cap intensity below 80% 1RM, do NOT convert the plan to machines only, and do NOT cut rep ranges to high-rep-only — none of these are justified by age alone.
- If age is absent, then apply the 18–39 defaults and add the age question to the ask-list.

## Sex → selection and prescription

- If sex is `female`, then keep exercise selection, %1RM bands, and rep ranges identical to the defaults — there is no evidence-based reason to change movement selection by sex.
- If sex is `female`, then optionally set `accessory` and `secondary` work 1–3 reps higher at the same %1RM (e.g. `3x12 @70%` rather than `3x10 @70%`) and rest 2–3 minutes rather than 3–4 between `secondary` sets, reflecting the better-supported finding of greater fatigue resistance at submaximal intensities; keep `primary` prescriptions unchanged.
- If sex is `male`, then apply the defaults with no modifier.
- If sex is `other` or `unspecified`, then apply the default (unmodified) prescriptions and do not ask about sex unless the athlete raises it.
- If the athlete asks for menstrual-cycle-phase periodisation, then note in a subroutine that the evidence for phase-based load prescription is weak and inconsistent, and prescribe the conservative default: a fixed weekly structure with permission to swap any session's `primary` to RPE-capped 3x5 at RPE 6 on a low-readiness day.
- If sex is `female` and the plan includes jumping, cutting, or landing in `conditioning` or `skill work`, then add 2 sessions per week of hamstring-biased `accessory` work (Nordic-style or Romanian deadlift, 3x6–8 at RPE 7) and 2x5 controlled landing reps in `warmup`, since knee-injury incidence is higher in these tasks.
- If the athlete reports being pregnant or postpartum, then do not write a modified plan from the profile alone — state that the plan assumes clearance to train unrestricted, and add the question to the ask-list.
- If any commonly-repeated sex difference is not in the two bullets above (upper-body-only training, "toning" rep ranges, lower intensity ceilings for women), then do not encode it — it is not supported.

## Injury history → contraindications and substitutions

- If any past injury is listed, then treat every rule below as load management: adjust exercise selection, range, and intensity only, and make no statement about cause, healing, or treatment; if the region is currently painful, the plan defers to whatever the athlete has been cleared to do.
- If an injury region is `shoulders`, then avoid behind-the-neck pressing, wide-grip upright rows, and deep barbell bench below sternum contact; substitute neutral-grip dumbbell or landmine pressing at 3x8–12 at RPE 7; add 3x12–15 external rotation and 2x10 scapular retraction work twice weekly.
- If an injury region is `back`, then avoid maximal-load spinal flexion under bar and untested `1RM` deadlift attempts; substitute trap-bar deadlift or hip thrust at 3–4x5–8 at 70–80%; add 3x30–45s carries and 2x8 per side anti-rotation work twice weekly.
- If an injury region is `posterior chain`, then cap eccentric-heavy hamstring work at 2x6 at RPE 6 for weeks 1–2 and build to 3x8 at RPE 8 by week 6; substitute hip-hinge machine or cable work for stiff-leg deadlift; add 2x8 per side single-leg hinge weekly.
- If an injury region is `quads`, then reduce squat depth to the athlete's pain-free range rather than removing the squat, substitute leg press or split squat at 3x8–12 at RPE 7, and add 3x15–20 low-load knee-extension work twice weekly.
- If an injury region is `calves`, then avoid maximal plyometrics and unbroken running blocks over 10 minutes in weeks 1–3; substitute `conditioning` on rower or bike tracked by `cal`; add 3x12–15 straight-leg and 3x12–15 bent-knee calf work twice weekly.
- If an injury region is `chest`, then avoid maximal barbell bench and deep dumbbell flye stretch positions; substitute floor press or machine press at 3x8–12 at RPE 7; add 2x12 controlled-range pressing weekly and progress range before load.
- If an injury region is `arms` or `core`, then keep all direct work for that region at RPE 6–7 for weeks 1–2 with 2x10–15, and progress to RPE 8 only if the prescription was completed in full for two consecutive weeks.
- If an injury is listed with a year within the last 12 months, then start that region's items one intensity band lower (e.g. 65–72% instead of 75–85%) for weeks 1–3, then rejoin the normal progression.
- If an injury is listed with no region or with a region older than 3 years and no other flag, then make no substitution and add one relevant `warmup` subroutine only.

## Equipment → movement substitution

- If the pattern is squat, then descend the ladder: barbell back squat (barbell + rack) → front squat or safety-bar squat (barbell + rack) → goblet squat (dumbbells or kettlebells) → leg press or hack squat (machines) → Bulgarian split squat (bench + bodyweight) → banded squat (bands) → bodyweight squat and 3x10 per side step-up.
- If the pattern is hinge, then descend: barbell deadlift (barbell) → trap-bar or Romanian deadlift (barbell) → kettlebell swing or dumbbell RDL (kettlebells or dumbbells) → cable pull-through (cables) → back extension (machines) → banded good morning (bands) → single-leg hip hinge and 3x12 hip thrust off a bench, ending at 3x15 bodyweight single-leg RDL.
- If the pattern is horizontal press, then descend: barbell bench (barbell + rack + bench) → dumbbell bench (dumbbells + bench) → chest press machine (machines) → cable press (cables) → floor press (dumbbells or kettlebells) → banded press (bands) → push-up at 3x8–20, with feet elevated to add difficulty.
- If the pattern is vertical press, then descend: barbell overhead press (barbell + rack) → dumbbell or kettlebell overhead press → shoulder press machine (machines) → cable or band overhead press → pike push-up at 3x6–12.
- If the pattern is vertical pull, then descend: weighted pull-up (pull-up bar) → pull-up or chin-up (pull-up bar) → lat pulldown (machines or cables) → banded pulldown (bands) → inverted row at a low bar → 3x10 towel or door-frame isometric row at 3x20s.
- If the pattern is horizontal pull, then descend: barbell row (barbell) → dumbbell or kettlebell row → seated cable row (cables) → chest-supported machine row (machines) → banded row (bands) → inverted row at 3x8–15.
- If the pattern is carry or loaded core, then descend: sled push or drag (sled) → farmer carry (dumbbells or kettlebells) → cable Pallof press (cables) → banded anti-rotation hold (bands) → plank and side plank at 3x30–45s.
- If the pattern is `conditioning`, then descend: rower, bike, or treadmill tracked by `cal` or `distance` → sled pushes at 5x20 m tracked by `distance` → kettlebell swings at 10x30s tracked by `time` → bodyweight circuit of squats, push-ups, and burpees tracked by `time` at 8–15 minutes.
- If equipment is bodyweight only, then progress by leverage, range, tempo, and reps rather than load: track every item by `reps` or `time`, and write weeks as e.g. `3x8`, `3x10`, `3x12`, `4x12` with a note naming the harder variation to move to when the top of the range is clean.

## Training days per week → split

- If 1 day is available, then write one full-body session: `warmup` 2 items, `primary` squat or hinge, `secondary` one press and one pull, `accessory` 2 items, `conditioning` 1 item at 8–12 minutes, `cooldown` 1 item.
- If 2 days are available, then alternate: day 1 `primary` squat, `secondary` horizontal press and vertical pull; day 2 `primary` hinge, `secondary` vertical press and horizontal pull; both days 2–3 `accessory` and 1 `conditioning`.
- If 3 days are available, then run full-body A/B/C: day 1 `primary` squat, day 2 `primary` horizontal press, day 3 `primary` hinge; each day carries 2 `secondary`, 2–3 `accessory`, 1 `conditioning`.
- If 4 days are available, then run upper/lower twice: days 1 and 3 upper (`primary` press, `secondary` pull + press, 3 `accessory`), days 2 and 4 lower (`primary` squat then hinge, `secondary` unilateral, 2–3 `accessory`, 1 `conditioning` on lower days only).
- If 5 days are available, then run lower, upper, `conditioning`-led, lower, upper — with the third day carrying 1 light `primary` at 3x5 at 60%, 2 `accessory`, and 25–35 minutes of `conditioning`.
- If 6 days are available, then run push, pull, legs, twice through, with `conditioning` on the two pull days only and one session per week capped at RPE 7 across all items.
- If 7 days are available, then program 6 training days as above plus a seventh day containing only `warmup` and `cooldown` subroutines and 20–30 minutes of `conditioning` at RPE 4–5; never write 7 loaded days.
- If the requested day count exceeds what the experience rule allows, then write the lower day count and add a subroutine in `cooldown` on the final day named "Optional extra day" describing 20 minutes of easy `conditioning` and mobility.

## Plan length → block structure

- If plan length is 6 weeks, then use `accumulation 1-3`, `intensification 4-5`, `deload 6-6`.
- If plan length is 7 weeks, then use `accumulation 1-3`, `intensification 4-6`, `deload 7-7`.
- If plan length is 8 weeks, then use `accumulation 1-4`, `intensification 5-7`, `deload 8-8`.
- If plan length is 9 weeks, then use `accumulation 1-4`, `intensification 5-6`, `deload 7-7`, `intensification 8-8`, `realization 9-9`.
- If plan length is 10 weeks, then use `accumulation 1-4`, `deload 5-5`, `intensification 6-8`, `realization 9-9`, `deload 10-10`.
- If plan length is 11 weeks, then use `accumulation 1-4`, `deload 5-5`, `accumulation 6-8`, `intensification 9-10`, `deload 11-11`.
- If plan length is 12 weeks, then use `accumulation 1-4`, `deload 5-5`, `intensification 6-9`, `deload 10-10`, `realization 11-11`, `deload 12-12`.
- If plan length is absent, then write 8 weeks using the 8-week structure above.
- If any block is `deload`, then hold exercise selection constant and prescribe 50–60% of the prior week's sets at the same load, or the same sets at 60–70% of the prior load — never both cuts at once — and drop `conditioning` to one easy item.
- If the plan contains a `realization` week, then reduce `accessory` to at most 1 item per session, keep `primary` at 2–3 singles or doubles at 88–95%, and remove all `conditioning` except a 6-minute easy item.
- If experience is `beginner`, then never write a `realization` block — replace it with `accumulation` and place a `deload` in the final week only.
- If blocks are written, then verify every week from 1 to N is covered by exactly one block, with no gaps and no overlapping ranges, before emitting the plan.

## Conflict resolution

- If any two rules collide, then apply this precedence, highest first: (1) injury-history contraindications, (2) age-band rules, (3) equipment availability, (4) experience-level volume and complexity caps, (5) training-days-available split, (6) plan-length block structure, (7) goal weighting, (8) athlete's stated preferences.
- If an injury contraindication removes a movement that the top-ranked goal requires, then substitute the nearest pattern from the equipment ladder, keep the goal's rep and intensity band, and note the substitution on the item — never keep the contraindicated movement because the goal ranks high.
- If the requested day count exceeds the experience cap (e.g. 6 days requested with `beginner`), then experience wins: write 3–4 days and place the surplus intent in an optional low-intensity day.
- If equipment cannot support the top goal's prescription (e.g. `strength` at 85% 1RM with bodyweight only), then keep the goal's intent and change the metric — prescribe 4x5 at RPE 8–9 on the hardest available progression tracked by `reps`, and state in a `warmup` subroutine that %1RM targets are unavailable.
- If goal weights demand more weekly sets than the experience cap allows, then the experience cap wins and the surplus is taken from the lowest-weighted goal first.
- If the athlete requests a plan length whose block structure conflicts with a `beginner` level, then keep the requested length and swap `realization` for `accumulation`.
- If two goals of equal weight demand incompatible `primary` prescriptions on the same day, then the goal listed earlier in the ranked list wins for that day and the other gets the next session's `primary` slot.
- If a rule would produce fewer than 1 `warmup` item, 1 `primary` item, or 1 `cooldown` item in a session, then override it — every session must contain those three.

## What to ask the athlete

- If experience level is missing, then ask it first — it sets volume, complexity, progression style, and whether percentages can be used at all.
- If training days available is missing, then ask it second — it determines the split and total weekly volume.
- If equipment is missing, then ask which of barbell, rack, bench, dumbbells, machines, cables, pull-up bar, and cardio equipment they can access, because it decides every exercise in the plan.
- If goals or goal weights are missing, then ask them to rank their top three and put a rough percentage on the first — this sets all rep and intensity bands.
- If any injury is listed without a body region, then ask which region it affects and whether it currently limits any movement or range.
- If experience is `intermediate` or `advanced` and no recent 1RM or heavy set is known, then ask for a recent best set (weight x reps) on squat, bench, and deadlift so percentages are real rather than guessed.
- If age is missing, then ask it — it changes session length, warmup duration, power work, and rest-day count.
- If plan length is missing, then ask whether they want 6, 8, 10, or 12 weeks and whether any date (competition, trip, test) needs to land on a specific week.
